> *"True understanding is achieved by identifying common characteristics that exist in different things."*
> — Aristotle

A note on that epigraph, because it illustrates the method rather than merely decorating it: Baskaboo was not built on this line. The line was found afterwards. The working principle was arrived at independently, and the search for who had already said it came later. Section 3 explains why that search happens at all.

---

This document describes *how* Baskaboo works, not *what* it claims. It is written so that someone else can apply the method independently and arrive at the same result — or fail to, and say where.

---

## 1. What Baskaboo is for

Baskaboo connects. That is the whole of its ambition, and stating it precisely matters, because it is narrower than it is sometimes taken to be.

Baskaboo does not expect to discover new physics, invent new technology, or replace any existing field. It expects to supply a map of connections between fields — and for the discoveries to be made by others, working in their own domains, using that map.

This scope has a consequence worth being explicit about: when a Baskaboo document touches dark energy, or entropy, or the structure of the atom, it is proposing a *reading* of how those things relate to each other and to structures elsewhere. It is not competing with the physics. Where a Baskaboo document has claimed more than that, it has been corrected.

## 2. Behaviour first, number second

The four-part structure is the visible part of Baskaboo, and it draws the most attention. It is not the important part.

What does the work is the **behaviour of each element and the relations between them**. Pits, Mits, Klop and Laram are not four slots to be filled. They are four characters with distinct dispositions: what each one wants, what it does when it meets another, what it produces, what it destroys. Once those behaviours are held clearly, recognising them in an unfamiliar domain becomes straightforward — the number four follows from the behaviours rather than being imposed on them.

This ordering matters for anyone testing the framework. Asking "does this domain have four things?" is the wrong question and will produce false matches, because groups of four are common. The right question is: **do these elements behave the way the Voices behave, and stand in the same relations to one another?**

## 3. Idea first, reference second — and why that order matters

The working order is: think of something, then search for whether someone has already said it.

The reason is not attribution, and it is not credibility. It is the opposite of borrowing. **The convergence is the evidence.**

Baskaboo's claim is that apparently unrelated things share a structure. So when a position arrived at independently turns out to have been reached before — by Aristotle, by Plato, by Schopenhauer, by chaos theory, by the four arithmetic operations — that is not Baskaboo leaning on their authority. It is the claim doing exactly what it predicts: separate minds, separate centuries, separate methods, converging on the same shape. Each such convergence is a data point for the thing being asserted.

Several of the framework's central components were built this way. The two worlds and the two bridges between Pits, Mits, Klop and Laram — later expressed as Flip, Square, Flip-back, Root — were assembled by looking for particular *characteristics*. Only afterwards did it become clear that what had been described overlapped with Plato's separation of forms and matter, and with the chaos–order distinction. The names arrived after the behaviours, not before.

**The condition that makes this work.** Because convergence is the evidence, the quality of each individual convergence carries weight. A loose match does not merely fail to help — it discounts the strong ones alongside it, since a reader who finds one weak link will assume the rest are of the same kind. Modern search and AI make it trivially easy to find something that *resembles* any given idea; the discipline is in rejecting the resemblances that do not hold on inspection. A located reference is a candidate connection until it has been examined, not a confirmation on arrival.

## 4. Vertical reading


Most fields develop horizontally: physics leads to more physics, psychology to more psychology. This produces precision within a domain and leaves the relationship *between* domains unexamined.

Baskaboo reads vertically. It asks whether the arrangement found in one domain appears in another domain that never borrowed from it — not as analogy, but as the same functional structure expressed in a different vocabulary.

The Magic Match Table is the instrument for this. It is not a summary of conclusions; it is a search directive. The order is:

1. The table indicates a position — a function that should exist in a given domain
2. We go and look for it
3. What is found, or not found, is recorded

This is a claim about method, and it is checkable: the order of documents in the repository shows which came first.

**Why the reading has to be vertical.** The horizontal structure of the classical world is its surface. Reading along it produces detail, not depth — more of the same layer. If there is a code underneath, it cannot be found by moving further along the layer it generates. It has to be reached by cutting across.

This is the sense in which Baskaboo works quantumly inside a linear world. Vertical reading looks irrational from within any single field, because within a single field it *is* irrational — it ignores the boundaries that define the field. That is not a flaw in the method; it is the method. The boundaries are a property of the rendered surface, not of what produced it.

## 5. Completing and correcting

Where a domain has settled on three elements, Baskaboo predicts one of two things: a fourth is missing and the framework says what it does, or the triad is wrong and one of the three is not what it is taken to be.

This has been applied already — a fourth term added to Freud's Id–Ego–Superego, a fourth stage to the Hegelian dialectic.

This is the strongest form of the claim, because it can fail. A framework that only matches existing groups of four is unfalsifiable. A framework that says what is missing can be checked and can be wrong.

### The unwritten part: the autonomy criterion

> **⚠ This section is not yet written. It is the gap this document exists to expose.**

Baskaboo holds that four is complete. So where a domain presents five or six elements, the framework claims the surplus are not autonomous — they are parts or aspects of one of the four.

**This claim is only meaningful if there is a criterion for autonomy that does not reduce to "otherwise there would be five."** Without that, every count confirms the framework and nothing tests it.

The precedent is Mendeleev, who did not only predict missing elements — he corrected atomic weights that had been measured wrongly, because they did not fit the structure. He could do this legitimately because he had an independent criterion: the periodicity of properties. He was not saying "this is wrong because it doesn't fit." He was saying "this is wrong, and here is what you will find when you measure it again."

**What the criterion must satisfy:**

1. **Stated before application** — written and committed before being applied to any specific case, so it cannot be adjusted to produce the desired count
2. **Independent of the number four** — applicable without knowing what total it will produce
3. **Reproducible** — another reader applying it to the same domain reaches the same verdict, or can show why not
4. **Able to fail** — there must be a possible finding, five genuinely autonomous elements, that it would report honestly

**Two candidate forms:**

*Functional.* Each Voice performs one operation (−, +, ×, ÷). Two candidates performing the same operation are aspects of one Voice. Test: describe what each element does, independently of its name, and check for overlap.

*Structural.* FSFR is a closed four-step cycle. An element that does not occupy its own position — receiving from one and handing to another — is not a Voice. Test: place all candidates in the cycle and see which fail to take a position.

Given Section 2, the functional form is closer to how Baskaboo actually works, since behaviour is what the framework reads. But it is also more open to interpretation, and that is exactly where the criterion has to be tightest.

**How to derive it:** not abstractly. Reconstruct it from cases already decided — where Baskaboo has already judged something to be a part rather than a Voice, ask what was actually doing the work in that judgment. State that, then check whether it produces the same answer on a case not yet examined.

## 6. Independent audit

Every substantive document is submitted to multiple AI systems for review, and the reviews are published alongside it, including when critical.

One limitation is recorded here because it has been observed repeatedly: AI reviewers return uniformly high assessments across documents of very different rigour. Uniform enthusiasm regardless of the strength of a claim is one systematic tendency observed many times, not many independent confirmations. These reviews are useful for finding specific errors. They are not evidence that a claim is correct.

Errors found this way are corrected in place, and the correction is recorded rather than absorbed silently.

## 7. Falsification

`CALL_FOR_EXPERIMENTATION.md` specifies a decoherence experiment. If information does not scale as predicted, that result is published.

Its scope is stated precisely: it tests whether information scales quadratically. It does not demonstrate that reality is a simulation, and it does not test whether φ is the underlying constant. Those are separate claims requiring their own tests.

## 8. Publishing before certainty

Baskaboo publishes work that may contain errors rather than holding material back until it is certain, and asks reviewers to correct the record in public.

The reasoning: a framework developed in the open, with its corrections visible, is more honest than one that appears finished. The cost is that some published material will later be shown wrong. That cost is accepted.

What is not published: claims presented as demonstrated when they are assumed. The line Baskaboo holds is not between certain and uncertain — it is between labelled and unlabelled.
