## In one minute

We hand children plus, minus, times and divide as four buttons to memorise. They are four **characters**, and the personalities are not poetic licence:

- **Minus** is the only one that invents a new kind of number when told *impossible*
- **Plus** is the only one that has never once broken a rule of the world it lives in
- **Times** is the only one that shares itself with everyone inside the brackets
- **Divide** is the only one that can answer *there is no answer* — and the only one that keeps enough to rebuild what it was given

Each of those is checkable with a pencil in thirty seconds. Part One does the checking.

Then the four characters are tested against Baskaboo's Magic Match Table — classical, atomic, quantum. **Twelve cells, each marked for how well it holds: eight strong, three fair, one weak.** The weak one is named rather than hidden, and fixing it is an open invitation.

Two things in here are worth skipping ahead for.

**Part Three** applies Baskaboo's cycle to the operations and finds that Einstein's spacetime metric already contains one of its claims — the time term carries a minus sign where the three space terms carry a plus. Time is, in the mathematics of relativity, literally the inverted dimension.

**Part Five** does something unusual. At the one cell where the model fails, it **imposes the law anyway** and follows what breaks. What breaks is the standard interpretation of quantum mechanics. What stands in its place is Bohmian mechanics — a real, published, minority position in physics.

A framework that produces nonsense under pressure should be abandoned. One that produces Bohm is worth testing further.

# PART ONE — The Four Characters

We teach children that plus, minus, times and divide are four buttons on a calculator.

They are four **characters**. Every personality below is proved by something the operation actually does — checkable with a pencil in thirty seconds.

```
Pits    −    subtraction
Mits    +    addition
Klop    ×    multiplication
Laram   ÷    division
```

## Minus (−) — the rebel

**He does not care about fairness.**

```
12 − 5 = 7        but        5 − 12 = −7
```

Swap the numbers, the answer changes. Minus cares who goes first. He plays favourites.

**He breaks the rules of his own world.**

You have 3 sweets. Take away 5. In the real world: impossible.

Minus answers **−2** — and in doing so *invents a place where that answer can exist*. Negative numbers did not exist until subtraction insisted on them. Mathematicians called them "absurd numbers" for centuries and refused to use them.

This is the most rebellious act in arithmetic: an operation that, told *impossible*, builds a new world rather than stopping.

**He makes things simpler.** He removes. Whatever is unnecessary, he takes out.

> **For a child:** Minus is the one who asks *who's first?* — and who answers even when the answer shouldn't exist.

## Plus (+) — the perfectionist

**She is completely fair.**

```
12 + 5 = 17        and        5 + 12 = 17
```

Order is irrelevant to her. She is incapable of favouritism.

**She never breaks a single rule.** Whole numbers in, whole numbers out. Every time, forever. Minus escapes his world constantly; Plus has never once left hers.

**She never surprises you — and that is her sadness.** She only accumulates. She never turns anything into anything else. Add for a thousand years and you have a bigger pile of the same thing.

She works harder than any of them. She is never finished.

> **For a child:** Plus is the one who is always fair and never breaks a rule — and who can only ever make the pile bigger.

## Times (×) — the kind one

**She shares herself with everyone.**

```
3 × (4 + 5)  =  3 × 4  +  3 × 5
```

Two numbers inside the brackets. Times does not choose. She goes to **both**, gives each the same, leaves nobody out.

Mathematicians call this *distribution*. The word hides what is happening: fair sharing with everyone in the room. Try it with minus — `3 − (4 + 5)` — and it does not behave the same way.

**She creates what neither number had alone.**

```
3 × 4 = 12
```

Three is a length. Four is a length. Three times four is a **rectangle**. It has area. Plus makes a longer line; Times makes a **room**.

**She is Plus, grown up.** `3 × 4 = 4 + 4 + 4`.

**She holds the power to erase, and almost never uses it.** Multiply by zero and anything vanishes. Multiply by one and anything is left untouched. She has both, and uses the second constantly.

> **For a child:** Times is the one who shares with everyone inside the brackets, and who turns two lines into a room.

## Divide (÷) — the wise one

**She is the only one who can say "there is no answer."**

```
12 ÷ 0 = ?
```

Not zero. Not infinity. **No answer at all.** The others always answer. Only Divide knows some questions cannot be answered.

**She is the only one who gives two things instead of one.**

```
12 + 5 = 17                    one answer
12 − 5 = 7                     one answer
12 × 5 = 60                    one answer

12 ÷ 5 = 2, remainder 2        TWO answers
```

She hands back **what fitted** and **what was left over** — and does not pretend the leftover isn't there.

**She is the only one who remembers.**

This is the strongest single finding in this document, and it was not expected.

From `17` alone you cannot recover 12 and 5. From `7` alone you cannot. From `60` alone you cannot.

But from division's outputs you can:

```
quotient 2, remainder 2, divisor 5  →  2 × 5 + 2 = 12   the original, exactly
```

**Division with remainder is the only one of the four that keeps enough to rebuild what it was given.** Baskaboo assigns Laram the role of memory — the keeper of records. Division is, uniquely, the operation that can reconstruct.

**She is Minus, grown up.** `12 ÷ 3` means subtract 3 from 12 until nothing is left, and count.

> **For a child:** Divide is the one who gives two answers instead of one, and the only one honest enough to say *this has no answer*.

# PART TWO — The Structure

## They are two, at two ages

```
Minus  grows up into  Divide      (repeated subtracting)
Plus   grows up into  Times       (repeated adding)
```

Not metaphor — definition. Multiplication *is* repeated addition; division *is* repeated subtraction.

**The young ones ask "how much." The grown ones ask "how many times."**

`3 + 4` gives a quantity. `3 × 4` gives a *relation* — how one thing sits inside another.

The young count things. The grown count relationships between things. That is why the grown ones are **lighter**: a relationship weighs less than an object.

## The densities

| Operation | Voice | Density | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **+** | Mits | **very high** | Deals in concrete quantities, one unit at a time. Nothing is more literal than counting. |
| **×** | Klop | **high** | Still counts, but counts *relations*. A rectangle is thinner than the bricks that make it. |
| **−** | Pits | **low** | Removes rather than adds. Every application leaves less. |
| **÷** | Laram | **very low** | Keeps only the essence and discards the rest. The lightest thing an operation can do. |

Two things follow.

**Both lines lose density as they mature.** Even the growing line: multiplication is a grower, yet Klop is *lighter* than Mits. Because multiplication does not pile up — it **spreads out**. Space is thinner than the matter that generates it.

**Evolution, in this system, always means becoming less dense.** That rule has not been stated anywhere in Baskaboo before, and if it holds it connects directly to the framework's claims about a lighter, sparser Universe V3.

## Where each family ends: zero and one

```
7 − 7 = 0            the young family ends at NOTHING
7 ÷ 7 = 1            the grown family ends at ONE WHOLE THING
```

Baskaboo marks the world of Pits and Mits — chaos — with **zero**, and the world of Klop and Laram — order — with **one**. This was in the book years before this analysis.

> Growing up, in arithmetic as in life, is the difference between ending at nothing and ending at one.

# PART THREE — Applying FSFR to the Operations

Baskaboo's cycle is **Flip → Square → Flip-back → Root**. Applied to the operations and their classical correspondences, it makes four specific claims.

```
Energy  --FLIP-->  Matter  --SQUARE-->  Space  --FLIP-BACK-->  Time  --ROOT-->  new Energy
  −                  +                    ×                      ÷
```

## FLIP: Energy → Matter (− becomes +)

**The claim:** matter is the inverse of energy.

**In the operations:** subtraction removes structure; addition builds it. Exact opposites in what they do to form.

**In physics:** E = mc². Matter *is* energy — energy that stopped.

| Energy | Matter |
| :--- | :--- |
| no location | located |
| formless | formed |
| what can happen | what has happened |
| free | bound |

**Verdict: holds.** Standard physics stated in Baskaboo's vocabulary.

## SQUARE: Matter → Space (+ becomes ×)

**The claim:** space is matter, squared.

**In the operations:** length × length = area. Squaring a linear thing produces a spatial thing. This is not an analogy for how dimension is generated — it *is* how dimension is generated.

**In physics:** one object alone needs no space. Two objects require a *between*. Space is what matter requires in order to be multiple. General relativity goes further: matter determines the geometry of space.

**Verdict: holds.**

## FLIP-BACK: Space → Time (× becomes ÷)

**The claim:** time is the inverse of space.

**In physics — the strongest confirmation in this document:**

```
ds² = dx² + dy² + dz² − c²dt²
```

This is the metric of relativity, the equation defining the shape of spacetime. Three space terms carry a **plus**. The time term carries a **minus**.

Time is, in the mathematics of relativity, literally the negative-signed dimension. Baskaboo says time is the inversion of space. Einstein's metric says the same thing with a sign.

**And in character:** space separates things that exist together; time separates things that cannot exist together. You can go back in space. You cannot go back in time.

**Verdict: holds, and strongly.**

## ROOT: Time → new Energy (÷ becomes − again)

**The claim:** time returns as new energy, at a higher layer.

**Where it is supported:** in quantum mechanics, energy and time are a *conjugate pair*, bound by ΔE·Δt ≥ ℏ/2 — exactly as position and momentum are. Physics does treat energy and time as intimately paired, and Baskaboo places them adjacent across the closing step.

**Where it is not:** there is no standard physics in which time converts into energy. This step is Baskaboo's own claim.

**Verdict: unsupported, not contradicted.** It should be labelled as the framework's proposal rather than a reading of known physics. It is the weakest of the four.

## The difference between the two inversions

**Both flips are the same operation performed at different powers.**

```
FLIP:       −H  →  +H         inversion at the first power
FLIP-BACK:  +H² →  −H²        inversion at the second power
```

The first inversion acts on raw information. The second acts on information that has already been squared — already made spatial.

And they move in opposite directions through density:

| | from | to | direction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **FLIP** | Energy (low) | Matter (very high) | **descending** — into the material |
| **FLIP-BACK** | Space (high) | Time (very low) | **ascending** — out of the material |

**FLIP is the entry into the simulation. FLIP-BACK is the exit.**

One turns possibility into thing. The other turns thing into record. Same operation, opposite doorways.

# PART FOUR — The Twelve Cells

Marks: **Strong** = rests on a technical property. **Fair** = real but arguable. **Weak** = currently justified by feel.

## Minus — Pits

**Energy** *(Fair)* — Keep removing: shape, location, structure. What remains when nothing more can be taken is pure capacity to act. A good description, but several quantities could fit it.

**Bound Electron** *(Fair)* — Carries the **negative** charge; it is the minus of the atom. And *bound* matters: held, straining against the structure containing it. The charge argument is partly a convention of naming.

**Quantum Field** *(Strong)* — Take a region of space. Remove every particle, every excitation, everything removable. What remains is not nothing: it is the quantum field, still humming, still capable of producing anything. Subtraction carried to its limit lands exactly where Pits lives — the void that boils with potential.

## Plus — Mits

**Matter** *(Strong)* — Addition accumulates and never transforms. Matter is energy accumulated and locked in place.

**Proton** *(Strong)* — The proton count *is* the element. One: hydrogen. Two: helium. Six: carbon. The periodic table is built by addition, one at a time.

**Qubit** *(Weak)* — See Part Five. This is the cell that fails, and it fails informatively.

## Times — Klop

**Space** *(Strong)* — Multiplication is the operation that makes dimension. Addition makes longer lines; only multiplication makes room.

**Neutron** *(Strong)* — Protons repel each other; two alone cannot form a nucleus. The neutron adds binding without adding repulsion — it is why anything heavier than hydrogen exists. Not a builder: a **connector**. Klop's exact job.

**Entanglement** *(Strong)* — When you combine two quantum systems mathematically, you use a **product**. Entangled states are precisely those that cannot be separated back into parts. The mathematics of entanglement *is* a multiplication.

## Divide — Laram

**Time** *(Fair)* — Division returns what fitted and what was left over; each moment keeps a record and passes forward what has not resolved. The remainder argument is strong; the time argument leans on Baskaboo's own claim that time is memory.

**Free Electron** *(Strong)* — An electron that broke away. Division separates — and it is by leaving that it becomes able to carry current. He had to separate before he could transmit.

**Wavefunction** *(Strong)* — Every wavefunction must be **normalised**: divided by its own magnitude so probabilities sum to exactly **one**. A division whose destination is unity, in a world Baskaboo independently marked with 1.

**Tally: eight strong, three fair, one weak.**

### A limitation of this tally, stated by the reviewer

These marks have **no control group**, and that matters.

I never took a *different* set of four — random, or deliberately wrong — and tried with equal effort to justify it. If I had, and it also produced eight "strong" cells, then what the tally measures is not the structure of the table. It is my own facility at producing convincing justifications.

That facility is real and it is exactly the failure mode this project has spent months guarding against in other reviewers. I am not exempt from it.

**The test that would settle it:** give the same table to an independent reviewer with the cells *shuffled*, without saying so, and ask for the same cell-by-cell justification with marks. If the shuffled version scores comparably, this analysis is worth little. If it visibly struggles, the table has a property that a random arrangement does not.

Until that test is run, the tally below should be read as *one reviewer's reading*, not as a measurement.

### What the ancient system already contains

This is the part worth noticing, and it was not put there to fit anything.

Everything derived in Part Two of this document from the behaviour of arithmetic — the two lines, the evolution along each, the two worlds, the zero and the one — is **already present in the classical elements**, arrived at more than two thousand years earlier by people with no arithmetic argument available to them.

**The two lines, with the same maturation.** Alchemical and Platonic traditions hold that Air is a refined, spiritualised Fire, and that Water is a subtler, more adaptable Earth. That is exactly the structure this document derives from repeated subtraction and repeated addition:

```
Fire  →  Air        (Pits → Laram,  − → ÷)
Earth →  Water      (Mits → Klop,   + → ×)
```

**The same two worlds.** Air and Fire are the abstract pair; Earth and Water the material one. This is Baskaboo's ideas/matter split, and it is the same split that arithmetic produces when you ask which operations break out of their number system and which never do.

**The same zero and one.** The Fire–Earth interaction is traditionally the source of instability and chaos; the Water–Air interaction of balance and equilibrium. Chaos marked zero, order marked one — reached in this document by asking where each pair of inverse operations lands when it cancels.

Four independent structural features, all present before anyone connected them to arithmetic.

### An honest disagreement about how to read this

Where the author and the reviewer differ, and the difference matters for how much weight the row carries.

**The author's position:** the ancients never meant *earth* as soil or *fire* as flame. They were describing behaviour and were misread by a later science that could only see substances.

**The reviewer's position:** Empedocles and Aristotle did mean it materially — the medicine built on it drew blood from patients on the strength of literal humoral theory, and caused real harm. Reading the elements as a code of behaviour is a new interpretation, not a recovered one.

Both are recorded, because the disagreement is real and neither of us convinced the other.

What survives it either way: whatever the ancients thought they were describing, **the structure they left behind has the same shape as the one arithmetic produces.** That fact does not depend on which reading of their intent is correct.

## A second row that holds: the four elements of the human being

The framework maps a further set — Heart, Body, Blood, Breath — onto the same four. Applying the criteria used throughout this document, two of these cells qualify as **Strong**: they rest on a mechanism, not a resemblance.

**Blood = multiplication** *(Strong)*

Recall why Klop is the kind one:

```
3 × (4 + 5) = 3 × 4 + 3 × 5
```

She goes to everyone inside the brackets. She does not choose. Nobody is left out.

Blood does this biologically. It reaches every cell. It does not select. Whatever it carries, it distributes to all of them — and any part it fails to reach dies. Distribution, in flesh.

**Breath = division, with the remainder** *(Strong)*

Division is the only operation of the four that returns two things: what fitted, and what was left over.

Breathing is literally that. Inhale — the body **keeps the oxygen**, which is the quotient, what is used. Exhale — the body **releases the carbon dioxide**, which is the remainder, what would not fit.

Laram is the Voice of the remainder. Breath is the one bodily function that performs this separation on every cycle, continuously, for as long as a person is alive.

**What this adds to the framework's own claim**

Baskaboo already holds that Breath is the evolved Heart, as Laram is the evolved Pits, and supports it with the coupling of respiratory and cardiac rhythm. The operations give it a mechanism rather than a correlation:

**the heart subtracts; the breath divides.**

The remaining two cells — Heart as subtraction, Body as addition — read as **Fair** on the same scale. They are coherent, but they rest on character rather than on a specific mechanism, in the way that Energy and Matter do in the classical row.

## What would break it

If the cells were arbitrary you could shuffle them and tell an equally good story. Try:

- Multiplication at the quantum field, subtraction at entanglement → the product argument vanishes; nothing about removal produces connection.
- Division at the proton, addition at the free electron → protons are defined by counting upward, which is addition and nothing else; a free electron has separated, which addition never does.

The cells that resist shuffling are the strong ones.

# PART FIVE — Imposing the Law Where It Fails

The qubit cell does not hold. Rather than quietly dropping it, this section does what Baskaboo asks: **impose the law anyway, and see precisely what has to give.**

## What the model demands

Mits is definiteness, structure, accumulation. For the qubit to sit in that cell, the qubit must be a **definite unit** — something with a state, which can be counted and added.

Standard quantum mechanics says the opposite. A qubit in superposition is not definite. It has no value until measured, and on the standard interpretation that is a fact about the qubit, not about our knowledge.

**So the model and the physics contradict each other. Impose the model. What breaks?**

## What breaks

To force the qubit into the Mits position, you must hold that the qubit *does* have a definite state, and that superposition describes **our ignorance rather than the qubit's condition**.

That position has a name: the **hidden variables** interpretation — Einstein's position, the one behind *God does not play dice*.

And it has a known cost. **Bell's theorem**, confirmed experimentally many times since the 1980s, rules out *local* hidden variables. If particles have definite states all along, the influences between them must be **non-local** — acting across any distance instantaneously.

## What survives

Non-local hidden variables are **not** ruled out. There is a fully worked-out, mathematically consistent, experimentally indistinguishable interpretation of quantum mechanics built on exactly that: **Bohmian mechanics**, the pilot-wave theory.

In Bohm's picture, particles always have definite positions. A wave guides them. The wave is non-local — it responds instantly to conditions anywhere.

**A backend that always knows, guiding a frontend that appears uncertain.**

That is Baskaboo's architecture, expressed in the vocabulary of a real, published, minority-but-respectable interpretation of quantum mechanics — arrived at not by adopting Bohm, but by refusing to let one table cell fail.

## And the fan comes back

Elsewhere in Baskaboo there is an image: four coloured blades on a spinning fan, blurring into white. Reviewing it, I noted a limitation — *unlike a fan, where the blade really is somewhere, superposition has no hidden answer.*

**Impose the law and the limitation disappears.** If the qubit is definite and the blur is rendering, the fan analogy is not a simplification. It is literally correct. The blade *is* somewhere. You simply cannot see it from inside the frontend.

## What it would mean if this were right

1. **Superposition is epistemic, not ontic** — a statement about what the observer can access, not about what exists.
2. **Non-locality is fundamental**, not a paradox — exactly what you would expect if space is rendered rather than fundamental, since two points far apart on the screen may be adjacent in the code.
3. **The measurement problem dissolves.** Nothing collapses. The rendering layer resolves.

None of this is demonstrated. What is demonstrated is narrower and still worth having: **the model, imposed where it fails, does not produce nonsense. It produces a known position in the philosophy of physics.**

A framework that generated gibberish under pressure would be worth abandoning. One that generates Bohm is worth continuing to test.

# PART SIX — Where This Is Weak

The strongest objection, stated as strongly as it can be put.

**Two of the findings may be one fact wearing two costumes.**

Minus and Divide are the **undo** operations. Plus builds, Minus undoes it. Times builds, Divide undoes it.

- *Why does undoing make order matter?* Because building has no direction (3+4 is the same as 4+3), but undoing does — you undo something specific.
- *Why does undoing break out of the number system?* Because you can ask to undo something that was never built. "What plus 5 gives 3?" Nothing, in counting numbers. So the undo operation must invent an answer.

Both follow from the same source: these are the inverse operations. It is like saying "this man is tall" and "this man can reach the top shelf" — that is not two facts about him.

**What the objection does not cover:** why the grown-up operations are *lighter*, why Divide alone can reconstruct its input, and why the operation that closes the cycle is the one that generates φ. Those remain, and they are the parts worth testing.

# PART SEVEN — Teaching This to a Child

Five questions, no lecture.

**Which one is fair?** Write `12 + 5` and `5 + 12`. Then `12 − 5` and `5 − 12`. Let them work it out. They will say *fair* and *unfair* before you say anything. They are right.

**Which one breaks the rules?** "You have three sweets. Give away five." Wait for the objection. Then show them minus doing it anyway. The moment a child sees that an operation *invented a new kind of number* rather than accept "impossible," they have stopped memorising.

**Which one shares?** Draw `3 × (4 + 5)`. Show the 3 going to *both*. Then try `3 − (4 + 5)` and let them find that it does not behave the same.

**Which one can say "I don't know"?** Divide by zero on a calculator. The error is not the calculator breaking. It is the only operation honest enough to admit some questions have no answer.

**Which one leaves something behind?** Twelve sweets, five children. Two each — and two left over. The leftover is not a mistake. It is the only thing in arithmetic that says *not everything fits*. Every child understands this, because every child has been the one who did not get a sweet.

The instruction that matters most: *the wrong question is "are there four things here?"* Quadruples are everywhere and that question produces false matches. The right questions are:

- Which two elements can be swapped without changing anything, and which two have direction built in?
- Which pair accumulates, and which pair relates?
- Which element keeps a record and passes on a remainder?
- Which element can fail outright — have no answer at all?

If a domain has four elements but they do not sort this way, the match is decorative and should be dropped.
